# Metta Universal Brand Guide

- Version: 0.11 draft
- Prepared: 7 November 2026
- Last updated: 10 November 2026
- Owner for review: Community Partnership Department
- Proposed review cycle: annually and whenever an approved identity rule changes
- Applies to: Metta Welfare Association headquarters, centres, programmes, schools, staff, volunteers, vendors and approved partners

## 1 Purpose and authority

This guide establishes a shared identity and communication system for Metta Welfare Association (MWA). It combines the Management Committee-approved *Communication and Publicity Handbook* dated 30 August 2024, the earlier corporate identity manual, current official logo masters, the 2025/26 annual report, current Metta website practice and relevant external standards.

This is a consolidation draft, not a new policy approval. If this guide conflicts with an approved Metta policy, law, regulator requirement or written direction from Community Partnership, the higher-authority instruction wins. Community Partnership controls the corporate logo and public brand application.

Use the status labels **Approved**, **Verified**, **Observed**, **Recommended** and **Confirm before use** exactly as defined in the package README.

## 2 Brand foundation

### 2.1 Official name

- **Approved English legal name:** Metta Welfare Association
- **Verified Chinese name in the official logo:** 慈光福利协会
- **Approved short name after first reference:** Metta
- **Approved abbreviation for internal or technical contexts:** MWA

On first public reference, write “Metta Welfare Association (Metta)”. Use “Metta” thereafter. Do not call the organisation “Metta Welfare”, “The Metta”, or use MWA as the public-facing name unless space or context requires it.

### 2.2 Meaning of Metta

**Verified:** “Metta” is a Pali term associated with loving-kindness. Use this explanation only when relevant and avoid presenting a loose translation as the legal name.

### 2.3 Motto

**Approved:**

> Compassionate love to share,<br>
> Brightens hope to those we care.

Preserve the wording. Do not turn the motto into an unapproved campaign tagline.

### 2.4 Vision

**Approved:**

> Inspired by the Great Compassionate Vows of The Medicine Buddha, we aim to provide social services for the community, regardless of race or religion.

### 2.5 Mission

**Approved:**

> We are dedicated to delivering social service programmes for individuals and families by collaborating with our donors, partners, vendors and community organisations.

### 2.6 Core values

**Approved:**

- **Compassion** - treat clients with kindness and dignity.
- **Integrity** - uphold professional conduct and behave ethically.
- **Harmony** - treat people with equality and sensitivity, regardless of race or religion.
- **Respect** - show mutual respect and recognise diversity.
- **Continuous Improvement** - pursue service and performance excellence through learning and improvement.

Do not rewrite the formal values in governance or policy documents. Plain-language explanations may be used in public communication if the meaning remains intact.

### 2.7 Organisational promise

**Observed current expression:** “One Person. One Journey. One Metta.” appears in the 2025/26 annual report and current public communications. Treat it as a current narrative theme, not a replacement for the approved motto, vision or mission.

### 3.3 Naming rules

- Copy the official spelling, capitalisation, accent and spacing from the current owner source.
- Use “centre” and British English in prose.
- Preserve branded constructions: `Arts@Metta`, `ECO@Metta`, `Metta HomeCare`, `Metta Café` and `PreSchool` when these appear in approved names.
- Do not silently shorten a centre name in its first appearance. A defined abbreviation may follow: “Metta Day Activity Centre for the Intellectually Disabled (IDAC)”.
- Avoid describing Maitri School as a future school if it is already operating. The current website presents it as supporting students; older volunteer material still describes a future building. Verify the exact status and distinguish the school from the Maitri Building project.
- Centre totals are time-sensitive. Never infer a count by counting programmes, buildings or addresses.

## 4 Logo system

### 4.1 Primary mark

**Approved:** The MWA logo is the sole corporate mark representing the organisation and its centres. The primary composite contains the Metta emblem, Chinese name and English name.

Use an approved master file. Never redraw, trace, typeset or reconstruct the logo. Do not separate or retype its bilingual wordmark.

### 4.2 Approved master assets in this package

- `assets/metta-logo-official-master.svg` - scalable transparent vector.
- `assets/metta-logo-official-master.png` - transparent raster for office and web use.

The vector is preferred for print, large-format work and professional design. The PNG is suitable for presentations, documents and digital use when sufficient resolution is retained.

### 4.3 Minimum size and clear space

- **Approved print minimum:** the corporate logo must not be smaller than 20 mm wide on printed publications unless Community Partnership approves an exception.
- **Approved clear-space method:** keep at least the manual’s `X` space on all four sides, where `X` is the height of the bilingual name block shown in the identity manual.
- **Recommended digital minimum:** 120 CSS pixels wide for the full bilingual composite. Use a larger size where mobile rendering makes the name difficult to read.
- If the name cannot be read, use a larger mark. Never solve legibility by removing the wordmark without an approved asset and use case.

### 4.4 Correct use

- Keep the original aspect ratio.
- Use the full-colour master on white or a quiet, very light background.
- Use an approved monochrome or reversed master on dark or visually complex backgrounds.
- Maintain strong visual separation from text, photos, borders and partner marks.
- Place the logo once in a clear, stable position unless a template requires a repeated header or footer mark.
- **Page placement clarification:** the reviewed approved sources do not prohibit placing the complete Metta logo at the bottom or in a footer. Bottom placement is acceptable when the logo remains legible, meets minimum-size and clear-space rules, has suitable contrast and follows the approved template. A specific controlled template may still require another position.
- Obtain Community Partnership approval for external organisations, co-branding, sponsorship placement, special formats and exceptions.

### 4.5 Never do this

- Stretch, squash, rotate, skew, crop or mirror the logo.
- Change its colours.
- Add shadows, outlines, glows, gradients, animation or effects.
- Place it over a busy image without an approved holding treatment.
- Put it inside an unapproved shape.
- Translate, retype or rearrange the name.
- Create a centre-specific imitation of the emblem.
- Use a screenshot, social-media thumbnail or low-resolution copy as a master.
- Treat the legacy phrase “Caring For Our Communities” as a current universal tagline without confirmation.

### 5.2 Recommended interface tokens

Until Community Partnership approves exact organisation-wide digital swatches, use these only outside the logo:

- Primary blue: `#094FA5`
- Deep accessible blue: `#064A7F`
- Primary red: `#BF2B37`
- White: `#FFFFFF`
- Ink: `#14181D`
- Muted text: `#5B6066`

These are observed in the live Metta website and are implementation tokens, not replacements for Pantone logo colours.

### 5.3 Accessibility rules

- Meet WCAG 2.2 AA contrast: 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text.
- Do not use colour alone to communicate status or meaning.
- Test text placed on photos, gradients and coloured panels.
- Keep the logo itself unchanged; adjust the background or placement instead.

## 6 Typography

### 6.1 Approved corporate type

- **English:** Arial and Arial Narrow.
- **Formal letters:** 11 or 12 pt Arial, left aligned, A4.
- **Legacy Simplified Chinese typeface:** 文鼎中隶简, as recorded in the corporate identity manual. Confirm licensing and availability before distributing or embedding it.

### 6.2 Digital and campaign typography

The live 2026 website uses Manrope prominently, with Merriweather and Nunito in selected areas. The 2025/26 annual report uses a broader contemporary design system. These are **observed campaign/digital choices**, not approved replacements for Arial in corporate stationery or signage.

Recommended hierarchy:

- Corporate documents and cross-centre templates: Arial.
- Web interfaces: Manrope or Arial, with a robust fallback stack.
- Long-form editorial accents: Merriweather may be used sparingly if the template is approved.
- Chinese digital fallback: use an approved CJK font with broad device support after Community Partnership confirms the replacement for the legacy licensed face.

Never use an inaccessible decorative font for body copy, instructions, dates, donation information or safety content.

## 7 Voice, words and editorial style

### 7.1 Voice

Metta communication should be compassionate without pity; respectful and dignity-centred; clear, direct and specific; warm without becoming sentimental or manipulative; inclusive across race, religion, ability and age; accurate, transparent and accountable; and hopeful without promising outcomes that cannot be guaranteed.

### 7.2 Language standard

- Use Singapore/British English: `organisation`, `programme`, `centre`, `recognise`.
- Prefer short sentences, active voice and concrete actions.
- Use sentence case for headings unless an approved design uses another treatment.
- Write dates as `7 November 2026` in prose.
- Use one consistent time format within each product.
- State currency as `S$50` when the audience may be international.
- Expand abbreviations on first use.

### 7.3 People and service language

Use the term that is accurate for the service context and preferred by the person or programme.

- Prefer `people/persons with disabilities` in general public communication.
- Use `clients`, `residents`, `students`, `patients`, `care recipients`, `artists`, `apprentices` or `participants` when the relationship is known.
- Use `beneficiaries` mainly for formal reporting, governance or fundraising contexts where accurate.
- Avoid `the disabled`, `sufferer`, `victim`, `unfortunate`, `needy people`, `normal people`, `wheelchair-bound` and language that reduces a person to a condition.
- Use `uses a wheelchair`, `is autistic` or `person with autism` according to the person’s preference and context.
- Do not imply that a disability, age or illness makes a person inspirational merely for living their life.

### 7.4 Fundraising language

- Identify Metta, the intended use of funds and the relevant cause or programme.
- Keep every claim accurate and current. Never invent a tax status, matching arrangement, deadline, beneficiary count, cost or impact figure.
- Avoid guilt, pressure, fear and exaggerated urgency.
- Do not imply that a donation guarantees a specific result unless the programme can substantiate it.
- When a commercial fundraiser or participator is involved, include the disclosures required by Singapore charity rules.
- Treat donor identities and donation information as confidential unless valid consent and authority exist.

### 7.5 Examples

Prefer:

> Your donation supports personalised rehabilitation and social programmes that help seniors maintain independence.

Avoid:

> These helpless elderly people desperately need you. Donate now before it is too late.

Prefer:

> Metta supports people across different life stages through education, disability, employment and medical-care programmes.

Avoid:

> Metta saves the less fortunate from lives without hope.

## 8 Chinese and bilingual communication

- Use approved Chinese names and translations from the current translation source of truth; do not translate proper names from scratch.
- Do not mix Simplified and Traditional Chinese within one product unless the audience or source requires it.
- Preserve the logo as artwork; never replace its Chinese wordmark with typed text.
- Give English and Chinese comparable prominence when a product is declared bilingual.
- Translate meaning and action, not only words. Verify dates, prices, addresses, links, eligibility and calls to action in both languages.
- Obtain review by a competent human reviewer before public release of sensitive, medical, legal, safeguarding, fundraising or crisis content.
- If Chinese terminology conflicts with an older PDF, flag the conflict rather than silently choosing one.

## 9 Photography, video and illustration

- Show people with dignity, agency and authentic context.
- Prefer real activities and relationships over staged pity imagery.
- Obtain and record the appropriate consent or legal basis before capture and publication.
- Do not photograph inside centres without Metta consent.
- Do not expose names, records, diagnoses, addresses, screens, medication, case notes or other personal data in the frame.
- For children and people who may need supported decision-making, follow the relevant consent and safeguarding procedure.
- Write useful alt text describing the information or purpose of the image. Use empty alt text for purely decorative images.
- Add captions and photo credits when required.
- Do not use generative AI to create a photorealistic “beneficiary”, “client”, “patient”, “student” or testimonial that could be mistaken for a real person.
- Label conceptual AI imagery clearly when used, and obtain approval before public use.

## 10 Layout and universal design

### 10.1 Core layout principles

- Build a clear reading order with one primary message and one primary action.
- Use generous whitespace, consistent alignment and a restrained type scale.
- Keep body text left aligned; avoid long centred paragraphs.
- Make links meaningful out of context.
- Use real headings, lists, labels and tables rather than appearance-only formatting.
- Do not place essential instructions only inside images.

### 10.2 Digital accessibility

- Target WCAG 2.2 AA.
- Provide keyboard access and a visible focus state.
- Use semantic HTML and labelled form controls.
- Put errors beside the affected field and explain how to correct them.
- Ensure text can resize to 200% without loss of content or function.
- Caption video, provide transcripts for important audio and avoid unnecessary autoplay.
- Use descriptive page titles, headings and link text.

### 10.3 Print accessibility

- Use at least 11 pt Arial for sustained body copy where practical.
- Provide strong contrast and avoid thin type over images.
- Keep essential information away from trim and fold areas.
- Include a readable short URL near a QR code; a QR code must never be the only access route.
- Test QR codes at final physical size and from realistic distances.
- Supply accessible digital versions of important print documents.

## 11 Channel standards

### 11.1 Email

- Use an approved Metta sender and accurate reply-to address.
- Use a clear subject line and lead with the reader’s required action.
- Keep the full message understandable when images are blocked.
- Use live text, descriptive links and alt text.
- Confirm recipients, consent, suppression lists, sender identity, links and attachments before sending.
- A draft is not approval to send.

### 11.2 Social media

- Publish content relevant to Metta’s mission and values.
- Avoid controversial or sensitive topics outside Metta’s authorised position.
- Do not speak for Metta unless authorised.
- Never disclose confidential information or identify a client without approval and consent.
- Use captions and alt text.
- Escalate media queries and crisis issues to authorised spokespersons.

### 11.3 Website and forms

- Keep facts, contacts, eligibility, fees, donation use and programme status current.
- Use the current approved English/Chinese content workflow when applicable.
- Do not publish untested forms or payment journeys.
- Test keyboard use, validation, confirmation, email notifications, mobile layout and language routing.
- Never infer that a successful visual page load proves a submission or payment path works.

### 11.4 Presentations and reports

- Use the full logo on the cover and a restrained repeated mark thereafter.
- State reporting periods, sources and denominators.
- Label draft, internal, confidential and public versions correctly.
- Do not reuse stale counts or management details from prior reports.
- Provide source notes for material statistics and claims.

### 11.5 Signage and large format

- Use approved logo masters and Arial/Arial Narrow unless a campaign treatment is approved.
- Design for viewing distance, lighting and obstruction.
- Keep wayfinding language direct and bilingual where the audience requires it.
- Submit permanent fixtures and exceptional applications to Community Partnership.

## 12 Co-branding and partner use

- Obtain a current vector or high-resolution official logo from each partner.
- Follow each partner’s brand rules, minimum sizes and required disclosure language.
- Agree logo order, relative prominence, event naming and approval ownership before artwork begins.
- Do not imply endorsement, sponsorship or partnership beyond the written agreement.
- Keep Metta’s clear space intact.
- External use of the Metta logo requires Community Partnership approval.

## 13 Governance and approval workflow

### 13.1 Normal workflow

1. Define audience, purpose, channel, owner, deadline and required action.
2. Gather current approved facts, names, translations, consent and partner requirements.
3. Draft with this guide and the approved template.
4. Check brand, accessibility, privacy, factual accuracy, links and regulatory disclosures.
5. Obtain content-owner review.
6. Obtain Community Partnership review for public identity, logo, media, campaign and co-branding matters.
7. Obtain specialist review where required: clinical, safeguarding, legal, finance, fundraising, HR, data protection or crisis communication.
8. Record final-artwork approval and release the approved version only.
9. Archive the source, approvals, final file and expiry/review date.

### 13.2 Always escalate

- Logo exceptions or external logo requests.
- New taglines, sub-brands, programme names or colour systems.
- Public claims about outcomes, people served or donation use.
- Client stories, identifiable images or sensitive personal data.
- Clinical, legal, HR, safeguarding or crisis content.
- Media statements and responses.
- Paid campaigns, public fundraising appeals and partner commitments.
- AI-generated people, testimonials, statistics, translations or brand assets.

## 14 AI use standard

AI may assist with drafting, summarising, translating, resizing concepts, metadata, alt text, checklists and variants. It must not be treated as an approver or source of truth.

Every AI task must:

1. use this guide and the current source material;
2. distinguish supplied facts from suggestions;
3. preserve official names, logo artwork, mission, vision, motto and values;
4. avoid inventing statistics, quotations, testimonials, dates, contacts, services or approvals;
5. flag time-sensitive information for verification;
6. preserve privacy and exclude confidential or personal data unless the approved tool and purpose permit it;
7. require human approval before publishing, sending, printing, posting or changing a live system;
8. provide an assumptions and verification list with the draft.

## 17 Ownership and maintenance

- **Policy and identity owner:** Community Partnership Department, subject to Management Committee-approved policy.
- **Content owners:** the responsible centre, programme or corporate function for its facts and operations.
- **Specialist reviewers:** clinical, safeguarding, legal, finance, fundraising, HR and data-protection owners where relevant.
- **Bilingual review:** a competent human reviewer using the controlled Metta terminology source.
- **Review trigger:** annual review, approved policy change, identity-asset change, service-directory change, material regulatory change or documented recurring misuse.
- **Version control:** record changes in `CHANGELOG.md`; validate the package before every release; do not edit only the skill copy of the guide.
